# Zurich vault stocking recommendation

## Recommendation

Buy the **48 stones** listed below. The book weighs **56.79 carats**, costs **USD 185,152** wholesale, and projects **USD 361,179** of retail value (1.95×, **USD 176,027** gross margin).

## What this achieves

- Product mass in the window: **56.79 ct** across 48 settings
- Credit drawn: **USD 185,152** of 250,000 (74% of the line; **USD 64,848** left undrawn)
- Projected retail: **USD 361,179** at a 1.95× book multiple
- Gross margin at that multiple: **USD 176,027**
- Tray face used: **2,185 mm²** of 2,800 mm²

The objective is carats, not prestige. The mix is still a high-end book because included-grade stones are out, colour J is capped, and the 1.95× retail floor stops the solver filling the tray with the cheapest Fair commercial goods.

## Why these and not others

These constraints are tight — they are what stops a heavier book:

- **The display tray holds at most 48 settings** (achieved 48 vs bound 48)
- **Projected retail must be at least 1.95× wholesale cost** (achieved 1.95072 vs bound 1.95)
- **No more than 30% of the vault may be colour I** (achieved 0.291667 vs bound 0.3)
- **No more than 40% of the vault may be clarity SI1** (achieved 0.395833 vs bound 0.4)
- **No more than 40% of the vault may be clarity SI2** (achieved 0.395833 vs bound 0.4)
- **Colour J (noticeable tint) may not exceed 20% of the vault** (achieved 0.1875 vs bound 0.2)

The tray is full and **USD 64,848 of credit is unused**. A heavier book is not a money problem — it is a space problem. The solver filled every setting with the most mass it could buy without breaking the cut, colour, clarity and markup rules.

Stones left on the table are either worse carat-per-dollar than the chosen set, would push a risk bucket through its cap, would dilute the 1.95× retail floor, or never entered the candidate sample (only the ten densest stones in each cut × colour cell were offered to the solver).

## What would change the answer

The binding constraints above are the levers.

- **More settings.** 48 slots is the physical stop. Re-solving the same candidate set on a 60-stone tray lifts the book from 56.79 ct to **69.75 ct** (USD 226,165 wholesale) — twelve carats more, still inside the credit line.
- **A larger credit line does nothing until the tray grows.** Raising the line from USD 250k to USD 300k left total mass unchanged at **56.79 ct**. Money is not the bottleneck.
- **The 1.95× retail floor is sitting on the bound but is a weak lever.** Dropping it entirely adds only 0.12 ct. SI1/SI2 at 40%, colour I at 30% and colour J at 20% already keep the cheapest commercial goods out. Those caps, not the 30% cut rule, are what shape the mix — Fair is only 12% of the tray.
- **Include I1.** That would be the fastest way to more carats and the fastest way to a book this house should not put in the window.

## Assumptions

- Credit line set to USD 250,000. The dataset has no budget column; this is a working capital figure sized for a boutique vault, not a flagship.
- Display tray holds 48 settings (one stone each). Physical case capacity is not in the data; 48 is a standard high-end tray size.
- Tray face limited to 2,800 mm² of stone footprint (length × width). Dimensions x, y, z in the file are treated as millimetres.
- Only stones of 1.00 ct and above are eligible. Melee does not belong in a Zurich retail vault showcase.
- I1 (included) clarity is excluded as too risky for a high-end book. Twenty rows with zero or impossible x/y/z, and any stone with an axis above 15 mm or depth above 12 mm, are treated as measurement errors and dropped.
- Wholesale book has 53,940 rows. Candidates are the 10 best carat-per-dollar stones in each cut × colour cell among the eligible set, so every risk bucket stays represented without sending 19,000 binaries to the solver.
- Prices are USD wholesale as published. No CHF conversion, VAT, duty or setting-labour is applied.
- Projected retail is wholesale × a quality-adjusted multiple (base 2.00, then cut/colour/clarity lifts). The book must clear a 1.95× portfolio floor. This is a working proxy, not a Rapaport sheet.
- The prompt named two objectives (mass or margin). Mass is maximised; margin is the 1.95× floor so the recommendation is a vault of carats that still retail as a high-end mix.
- Risk categories: no cut above 30% of pieces (from the brief), no colour above 30%, no clarity above 40%, colour J capped at 20% because noticeable tint is a concentration risk in a luxury window.

## How to check this

The run directory holds three files: `workbench.duckdb` (the artifact), `model.py` (the formulation), and this report. Every selected `row_id` was joined back to `source` — not to the sampled `candidates` table — and every constraint was recomputed in pandas before the report was written.

```bash
riskon sql "SELECT name, business_rule, bound, achieved, slack, binding, satisfied FROM constraints"
riskon sql "SELECT * FROM solution WHERE selected = 1 ORDER BY carat DESC"
riskon sql "SELECT sum(carat) AS ct, sum(price) AS spend, count(*) AS n FROM solution s JOIN source USING (row_id) WHERE s.selected = 1"
```
